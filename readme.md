# [Ph.D. Dissertation from the Chinese Academy of Sciences] Research on Multicarrier Communication Technology in Waterborne Dual-Extension Channels

Orthogonal Frequency Division Multiplexing (OFDM) can convert frequency-selective fading channels into several parallel nearly flat fading subchannels, allowing the system to be balanced in the frequency domain by a single-tap equalizer, which has strong robustness against multipath propagation in wireless channels. Due to its simplicity of implementation, it holds a dominant position in current multicarrier radio communication systems. Unlike radio channels, hydroacoustic channels' time-varying nature simultaneously possesses high temporal selectivity, generating Doppler effects, thereby severely disrupting the orthogonality of OFDM subcarriers and leading to Inter-Carrier Interference (ICI). Moreover, hydroacoustic channels also have significant time delay spreads. Since the block inter-symbol guard interval in OFDM must be greater than the time delay spread, this significantly reduces its spectral efficiency. Therefore, the question arises whether OFDM remains the optimal multicarrier mechanism in hydroacoustic media.
OFDM is extremely sensitive to Doppler effects because it has very good frequency focusing, while time-frequency focusing is crucial for similar hydroacoustic channels with large time delays and fast time-variability. A linear filter bank multicarrier (L-FBMC) compensates for the frequency domain by extending the symbol duration, while also achieving time and frequency focusing. L-FBMC combined with Interleaved Orthogonal Amplitude Modulation (OQAM) can avoid Balian—Low Theorem by maintaining orthogonality only in the real domain to ensure perfect information reconstruction. However, the complex response of the channel will destroy the above-mentioned real-domain orthogonality. Since L-FBMC is not periodic waveform, its channel estimation and equalization are very difficult, making it challenging to extend to MIMO applications. Thus, the necessity of periodizing the filter bank to simplify equalization arises.
In recent years, with the broadband frequency division multiplexing (GFDM) becoming one of the candidate waveforms for 5G standards, circular filter bank multicarrier (C-FBMC) has gradually gained attention. Like OFDM, C-FBMC, as a periodic waveform, allows channel effects to be compensated by a single tap equalizer in the frequency domain. On the other hand, due to the use of filter banks, C-FBMC inherits the rapid time-frequency attenuation characteristics of L-FBMC in the discrete domain. Moreover, as a bridge between OFDM and single carrier frequency domain equalization (SC-FDE), C-FBMC is more flexible than extreme single carrier or multicarrier mechanisms in terms of time-frequency resource allocation, making it potentially better at addressing the temporal and frequency expansions of underwater acoustic channels.
This paper conducts a comprehensive comparison and analysis of various multicarrier technologies, with a particular focus on the time-frequency analysis and channel estimation of C-FBMC technology. Firstly, it defines and derives the closed-form expression for the biperiodic phase function for discrete periodic signals, providing a quantitative description of the time-frequency focusing properties of C-FBMC waves, revealing that their time-frequency focusing lies between OFDM and L-FBMC. Then, using the characteristic vectors of the DFT matrix as basis functions, a time-frequency invariant prototype filter is designed to minimize the Intersymbol Interference (ISI) and Interchannel Interference (ICI) under dual-extension channels. On the other hand, since C-FBMC waves are not orthogonal in both the time and frequency domains, it is difficult to achieve uninterrupted observation of the frequency domain pilots through interference-free observation. This paper proposes a method of using auxiliary data to synthesize interference-free frequency domain pilots, enabling most OFDM channel estimation algorithms to effectively port to the C-FBMC mechanism. Finally, an interleaved frame structure for OFDM and C-FBMC is designed, and a two-dimensional correlation-based channel estimation and equalization scheme is proposed, including a phase tracking algorithm for GFDM under variable Doppler. Simulation results show that this method can effectively reduce the system error rate, demonstrating its potential application in underwater acoustic dual-extension channels.
The entire text is divided into six chapters, with Chapter 1 serving as an introduction. The content of the remaining chapters is as follows: Chapter 2 covers OFDM foundations. Starting from the time-invariant channel model and referencing the characteristic decomposition theory of circular matrices, it reveals a core contradiction for subsequent sections of this paper: mathematically strict frequency domain single-point equalization only holds true for periodic transmission waveforms. Moreover, only the signal structure of OFDM allows for uninterrupted frequency domain pilot observation. Next, the mainstream OFDM channel estimation methods are introduced; finally, the time-varying channel model is introduced to reveal that OFDM's extreme sensitivity to Doppler is due to its poor frequency focusing properties.
Chapter 3 designs the window-forming differential OFDM system and studies its application in hydroacoustic dual-extension channels. Window forming technology can accelerate the sidelobe attenuation of OFDM spectrum, thereby improving its frequency focusing. However, the addition of windows reduces the spectral efficiency of the system. To address this, differential modulation and demodulation are introduced. On one hand, it compensates for the data rate loss caused by windowing; on the other hand, it enhances the robustness of demodulation in weak time-varying channels. This chapter compares the designed system with two mainstream algorithms in current underwater OFDM communication and analyzes their respective advantages and disadvantages.
Chapter 4 continues the content of Chapter 3. Due to the increased OFDM symbol duration caused by windowing, and the need for inter-block guard intervals in windowing methods, its spectral efficiency is highly constrained. To address this, a more generalized linear filter bank multicarrier communication (L—FBMC) approach is introduced. Starting from FFT, the basic concepts of filter bank multicarrier are extended. The Balian—Low theorem is then introduced, which is the core that leads to the contradiction among time-frequency density, waveform orthogonality, and time-frequency focusing, guiding the design of all multicarrier systems. Next, a special orthomodulation method that theoretically achieves both time-frequency focusing and 100% spectral efficiency is introduced, combining Interleaved QAM (OQAM) modulation with L—FBMC. Finally, the challenges of channel estimation for L—FBMC/OQAM are analyzed, while introducing existing research on L—FBMC in underwater acoustic communication. Based on this, simulations are conducted to compare the bit error rate performance of this mechanism with traditional OFDM under dual-extended channels, proving that without complex channel estimation and equalization techniques, L—FBMC struggles to handle large channel dual-extensions, laying the groundwork for the next chapter on the cyclic filter bank multicarrier mechanism.
Chapter 5: After the introduction of a cyclic filter bank multicarrier mechanism C-FBMC, its periodicity and cyclic characteristics can compensate for the limitations of linear filters, allowing channels to be equally balanced by single tap equalizers in the frequency domain as is the case with OFDM systems. This chapter will investigate an issue that has not been widely addressed in previous work, namely the generally accepted notion that cyclic filter bank waveforms possess time-frequency focusing properties. It will define and derive closed-form expressions for the biperiodic fuzzy function for discrete periodic signals, revealing that the time-frequency focusing nature of C-FBMC actually represents a compromise between OFDM and L-FBMC. Furthermore, utilizing the characteristic vectors of the DFT matrix as base functions, a time-frequency homogeneous filter capable of achieving the minimum time-bandwidth product will be synthesized. Subsequently, by setting up auxiliary data, C-FBMC will have a pilot structure in the frequency domain similar to OFDM, enabling most OFDM-designed channel estimation algorithms to be ported to the C-FBMC system. Finally, the chapter introduces the frame structure for two-dimensional channel estimation using GFDM data block interleaving with OFDM, as well as the GFDM phase tracking algorithm for time-varying Doppler, and evaluates the performance of this mechanism through simulations.

Chapter 6: Conclusion and Future Prospects, summarizes the main content and innovations of the entire paper, points out the shortcomings in the work, and outlines future research plans.
Innovations:
1. The windowing-shaped differential OFDM system proposed in this chapter significantly improves the bit error rate performance of acoustic OFDM systems based on two-dimensional sparse channel BP estimation and MMSE equalization, at a very low complexity. Although windowing and differential modulation are mature technologies in signal processing and communication, their combination is not common. This chapter proves experimentally that the above combined scheme can effectively be applied in acoustic dual extended channels, holding significant engineering value;
2. In the field of wireless communication, linear and cyclic filter bank multicarrier techniques have been two popular research directions in recent years. Due to their relatively short history, research on these topics has not been as extensive or profound as that on OFDM. In the context of underwater acoustic communication, based on the limited literature review conducted by the author, this paper is the first systematic study of cyclic filter bank multicarriers in the context of strong dual-extension channels. It includes time-frequency characteristics analysis, filter design, and channel estimation and equalization algorithms, thus offering insights for future research in underwater acoustic communication.
3. Regarding the key research object of this paper, cyclic filter bank multicarriers, even in the field of radio communication, there are still some research blind spots, such as analyzing their time-frequency focusing characteristics. This paper directly starts from discrete and periodic signals, defines and derives the closed-form expression of the biperiodic fuzzy function, serving as a benchmark for continuous time fuzzy functions. It quantitatively measures the time-frequency focusing performance of cyclic filter bank multicarriers, revealing that not every group of waveforms in C-FBMC has time-frequency focusing properties, especially that the first subsymbol at the edge needs special treatment. This analysis has certain guiding significance for the design of cyclic filter bank communication systems;
4. Using DFT feature vectors as base functions, a discrete filter capable of achieving minimum time-bandwidth product was synthesized. This design reduces the dispersion range of the filter's time domain energy, which in turn improves the frequency focusing properties of several subsymbols at the edges. Moreover, during this process, interesting characteristics of HGL vectors corresponding to the biperiodic fuzzy function were obtained, analyzing its similarities and differences with continuous time Hermite functions. As a bridge connecting continuous and discrete analyses, it has certain inspiration for other signal processing applications such as image processing and signal reconstruction.
5. The method of synthesizing interference-free frequency domain pilots using auxiliary data under the GFDM and C-FBMC/OQAM mechanisms is proposed, along with the mathematical expression for the condition number of the coefficient matrix. This method allows both systems to have the same pilot structure in the frequency domain as OFDM, thus enabling most OFDM-designed channel estimation algorithms to be ported to GFDM and C-FBMC/OQAM systems.
6. An interleaved frame structure for OFDM and GFDM is designed, and a phase tracking algorithm for GFDM under time-varying Doppler is proposed. By combining the advantages of OFDM in channel estimation and GFDM in robustness against Doppler shift, simulation results under time-varying Doppler extended channels demonstrate that this structure can effectively reduce the bit error rate of traditional OFDM frame structures.
